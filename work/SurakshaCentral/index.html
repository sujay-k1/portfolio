Strengthened child-safety risk identification by standardizing audits of Rescue Homes

About

Aangan created a planning and review framework for childcare institution audits. The challenge was to turn a paper-heavy field process into a more reliable digital workflow for data capture, analysis, and follow-through.

Project Overview

This work focused on digitizing the audit tool, simplifying the experience through usability-led refinements, and extending the effort into a broader brand and system foundation for Aangan's growing product ecosystem.

Introduction

Aangan is tackling the issue of abuse and enhancing child safety.

Aangan developed a planning and review tool based on the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015, and Model Rules, 2016. Under this program, Aangan's volunteers and inspectors visited Rescue Homes to conduct audits, manually replicating data from physical forms into a digital format. This process allowed for data storage and analysis, but it led to accuracy and reliability issues and was time-consuming for generating reports and deriving insights.

Aangan's auditing framework

Data collection and report generation for CCIs

Aangan audited Child Care Institutions periodically. They gathered the data and insights for each CCI. They aggregated the data at district as well as state-level, and worked with officials of Women & Child Development state departments to chart out plans for improvement.

Here's how their auditing worked:

  • Aangan evaluated Child Care Institutions(Rescue Homes) across parameters like Management & Staff, Nutrition, Psychological care, etc.
  • Each parameters had multiple in-depth criteria, example: Do staff members stay in the living area at night, are there women staff members for girls?
  • Volunteers go through documents, make observations, and talk to children and staff to scrutinize against each criteria, example: maybe the children are left unaccompanied at night
  • They assign scores to each criteria, identify redflags, and prepare a report containing a final score and recommendations

Digital audit tool

Aangan’s framework simplified to a digital audit tool.

Over time, Aangan had a rich repository of observations against criteria. We derived common themes to create a tiered system of score: 0, 0.5, and 1. For example, a children being left accompanied corresponds to a score of 0, if there are few such occurences, it corresponds to 0.5, etc. We also retained the option of recording new observation against each scoring tier. We called them "Qualifiers".

First iteration

  1. Volunteers first selected a score, then they pick the qualifier that holds true
  2. Persistent drop-down to switch between different parameters at any time
  3. Process used to evaluate the CCI for credibility of the audit
  4. The section of the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015 that mandates meeting the criteria are mentioned in case the Surveying team has to justify the criteria.

Refinement strategy

The workflow required further simplification.

We collaborated with the Aangan management to take refinement decisions.

UI changes:

  • Removed selection of recording the process of evaluation, we added the required process instead
  • Removed unnecessary filters.
  • Edited text to make more comprehensible.

Scoring criteria:

  • We removed scores from the UI: Now, volunteers would select the qualifiers and the scores would get assigned behind the scene.
  • Simplified internal evalation to assign only a score and not a qualifier

Feature based changes:

  • Auto-saving: Removed the complex choice of selecting exiting without saving, saving and exiting, and cancelling.
  • Maintaining a universal set of CCIs at the back-end to ensure data collection accuracy and analysis.
  • Redesign of Summary and Dashboard to make more concise and less information heavy.

Design pattern and Design system

Unifying Aangan’s product offerings to enhance operational efficiency and establish a strong digital presence in the community.

As Aangan planned to launch multiple products, a unified brand strategy became essential. They aimed to remain a parent brand in the community while also creating a distinct brand that portrays a digital personality. To clearly communicate the relationship between the brands and emphasize the association with the parent brand, we opted for an endorsed branding approach. This strategy also allowed us to centralize common products, such as a portal serving as a unified hub for configuration, back-end management, and data visualization.

We built a library of UI components and interactions that could be reused. They were designed in a way other products could carry their own unique identity, and at the same time feel parts of the same family.
